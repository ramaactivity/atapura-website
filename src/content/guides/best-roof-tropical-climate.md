If you have moved to Indonesia from a cooler country, the roof will shape how your house feels more than almost anything else. Here is what measured studies show, rather than what sellers claim.

## The roof is where the heat comes in

A review of passive cooling research for tropical houses in Southeast Asia put the roof at about **70% of total heat gain**. That makes roof decisions more important than walls or windows for comfort.

## What measurably lowers the temperature

| Measure | Result | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Ventilated space under the roof | Indoor air 1.2 °C cooler on average | North Sulawesi, zinc roof |
| Ceiling with aluminium foil | Indoor air 0.5–2 °C cooler than a bare roof | Small-scale test models, Indonesia |
| Lighter roof colour | Maroon zinc roofs ran more than 1 °C hotter than silver or blue | Manado |
| Red tiles changed to white | Peak roof surface up to 16 °C cooler, cooling energy down up to 13% a year | Malaysia, simulation |

The roofing material matters as well. In North Sulawesi, average attic temperatures were 33.7 °C under plain zinc, 33.02 °C under heat-resistant painted zinc, and 31.7 °C under a coconut-leaf roof.

## Rain noise

Heavy tropical rain on a thin roof can stop a conversation. In a laboratory rain test following ISO 10140, a single-skin trapezoidal metal roof measured 74 dB; a build-up with glass wool and damping tape brought it to 52 dB. Whatever you choose, ask what sits between the rain and the room.

## Where asphalt shingles fit

Asphalt shingles are nailed onto a solid deck of 12 mm plywood or GRC board over the frame, with a waterproof underlayment between. That deck damps rain noise and slows heat moving down, and the shingles can follow complex roof shapes down to walls. They need a minimum pitch of 10–15° depending on brand, and their colour affects heat like any roof. Costs and brands are in [asphalt shingles in Indonesia](/en/guides/asphalt-shingles-indonesia).
