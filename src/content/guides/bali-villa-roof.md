A villa roof in Bali does more work than a roof in most of the places our clients come from. The numbers explain why.

## Bali's climate in numbers

| BMKG Denpasar station, 2025 | Value |
|---|---|
| Total rainfall | 3,222 mm |
| Rain days | 175 |
| Wettest months | January (577 mm), December (556 mm), February (433 mm) |
| Driest months | October (44 mm), April (74 mm), August (82 mm) |
| Average / highest temperature | 28.1 °C / 36.2 °C (February) |
| Average humidity | 79.5% |

## What that means for the roof

**Heat and humidity together.** Warm, damp air collects under the roof. The US asphalt roofing manufacturers' association (ARMA) recommends a net free ventilation area of at least 1/300 of the attic floor, 1/150 preferred. Researchers at Lawrence Berkeley and Oak Ridge national laboratories note that heat build-up from poor ventilation speeds up the ageing of asphalt shingles.

**Dark stains in humid climates.** On asphalt shingles, dark streaks are most often caused by algae (Gloeocapsa magma), according to the same weathering review. They are mainly cosmetic; clean gently and avoid high-pressure washers, which strip the protective granules.

**Where roof planes meet.** Villas often have hips, valleys, and roofs meeting walls. These are the points that need extra underlayment and flashing; the shingles themselves rarely leak in the field of the roof.

**Plan around the dry months.** The deck must be dry when it is laid. October was the driest month in Denpasar in 2025, while December to February is very wet.
